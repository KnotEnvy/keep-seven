# Pitch A — THREE TOLLS

*Angle: story and atmosphere first. The player should remember a place and a feeling.
Combat punctuates; it does not dominate.*

Working title of the game: **The Long Assize**. Working title of this stage: **Three Tolls**
(the town is called Tarry).

All names, terms and lines in this document are original to the project. A quick check of
the coined terms against the series' official glossary found no collisions (see Risks, 11).

---

## 1. The pitch in one breath

**Logline.** A circuit judge of a dead court, eleven years behind the man who re-measured
the world, walks into a swept and empty well-town whose people voted to become the staff
of the machine under their well. To give the town back to itself she must spend the last
three bell-metal rounds she was saving for him, and take the town's water away with them.

**The feeling.** Somebody has swept every porch in town and nobody lives there. The trough
is full, in a country where a full trough is a thing to be afraid of. The horror in Tarry
is not ruin. It is *tidiness*: a town that is being kept.

**The one mechanic that carries the story.** Every enemy in the stage but one is a
townsperson wearing a ceramic faceplate. A body shot kills them. **A shot that breaks the
plate frees them: they sit down in the dust and breathe.** Precision with the revolver is
mercy. There are exactly thirty-one of them. When the stage ends at blue hour, the town's
windows are lit for the ones who came home, and the end card reads:
*"On the roll: 31. Came home: 22."*

**Tone statement.** Melancholy and patient, never grim. Late-afternoon light, long mauve
shadows, glass-clear ceramic bells turning in the wind. The narrator is dry, practical and
counts things: rounds, porches, coats, steps. Terrible things are stated flatly. Nobody in
this stage is a monster, including the machine, which is polite, and the man she is
chasing, who is courteous and has never once hurried. Dread comes from order, consent and
arithmetic. The gun is loud in a quiet place, and the game makes the player decide what
kind of loud.

**Why it fits the pillars.**

| Pillar | How this pitch serves it |
|---|---|
| The gun is the star | Where the bullet lands is the moral content of the stage. Puzzles are played on the gun like an instrument. The special round is the loudest, most beautiful sound in the game. |
| A world that has moved on (internal pillar label; never player-facing) | A frontier town built in the shade of a dead aqueduct, on top of a utility that still keeps its hours. Signs whose distances have been corrected upward, twice. |
| Story through place | A swept street, a vote scratched on a wall, thirty-one coats on thirty-one pegs, lit windows you can count. Under 450 words of reading on the critical path. |
| Puzzles use the gun and attention | Four puzzles: a tune shot on bells, sunlight let in by bullets, a timing shot through a fan, six targets on one cylinder. |
| 60 fps on integrated graphics | One humanoid rig for both human enemy types, one rigid machine brute, a rigid mechanical boss, one street reused at a second time of day, at most 7 enemies alive. |

## 2. Lexicon (original; ten terms)

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **The Assize** | The fallen order of circuit judges who rode between the well-towns carrying the law. A member is a **rider**. (Ordinary English for a travelling court; here it is the order's name.) |
| **Toll** | A round cast from the Assize's court bell. Lead does not hold what the Assessor ties; a toll does. Also what a thing costs. She has three. |
| **The Assessor** | The pursued. Once a clerk of the court. He re-measures things, and they stay re-measured. He leaves survey stakes, ribbon and notes. |
| **The Wick** | The hairline of violet light on the western horizon, a hair off vertical. Everything the Assessor does bends toward it. |
| **Plumb / off-plumb** | The world's words for right and wrong. A thing the Assessor has touched hangs off-plumb. A toll "rings plumb". |
| **Knot** | The Assessor's lock: a surveyor's plumb bob on violet ribbon, hanging at an angle no weight should hang at. Bullets ring flat off it. |
| **The Kept** | Tarry's people after the vote. Collared and plated by the station, they keep its hours. Two kinds are met: **Tenders** and **Rodmen**. |
| **Strike plate** | Pellam Deepworks' manual override: a bone-white ceramic disc with an ochre ring. STRIKE TO RELEASE. It is the stage's "shoot here" mark, and it is also what the Kept wear on their faces. |
| **The Flume** | Folk name for the dead Pellam aqueduct whose piers march west to the Wick. Tarry stands in the shade of one pier. |
| **Chain** | The unit of distance (a surveyor's chain, about 20 m). Road signs give distances in chains. The numbers have been painted out and raised. |

**Courtesy** (used four times in the stage, never more): *"Shade to you."* The reply is
*"And water after."* The town gate says the first half. Nobody says the second until the
final title card.

**The maker.** Pellam Deepworks, with the plumb-line mark, as proposed in
`docs/research/art-tone.md` 4.4. House voice: calm, paternal, certain.

## 3. Who, what, and what it costs

**The player** is Rider Enid Harrow, last of the Assize. Seen only as hands, a dark coat
cuff and a blued revolver. She does not speak aloud. The narrator speaks of her in the
past tense and the third person ("she"), and what the narrator mostly reports is what she
counted.

**Backstory (141 words).**

> The Assize rode circuit between the well-towns: one judge, one horse, one bell-hall where
> the roads met. Its riders carried the law in a book and enforced it with six rounds. Then
> a clerk of the court, the Assessor, began correcting the surveys. Roads grew longer than
> their maps. Wells went off-plumb. Riders set out for towns that were no longer where they
> had been left. The night the hall burned, the last of the bench melted the court bell into
> twelve rounds, because lead had been tried on him and had not held. Enid Harrow carried
> them out. That was eleven years ago. Nine are spent. She has three tolls, a revolver, and
> his trail, which runs west along a dead aqueduct toward a line of violet light the towns
> call the Wick. He walks. He has never once hurried.

**What she pursues.** The Assessor, four days ahead by the ash in his fires. He leaves
things on purpose. In this stage he has left a whole town.

**What this stage costs her.**

1. **All three tolls.** Tarry is not a trap to kill her. It is a trap to disarm her. Each
   of the three places she cannot pass is tied with one knot. He has priced the town at
   exactly what she carries, and the player pulls the trigger on each payment.
2. **Her standing.** Thirty-one people voted for what they have. Four voted against.
   Nobody asked the Assize. She ends the bargain anyway, and the town's only water with it.
3. **Whatever she could not shoot cleanly.** The number on the end card.

## 5. Zone by zone

The stage runs east to west, toward the low sun and the Wick. Overall footprint about
230 m (E–W) by 90 m (N–S). The underground sits directly beneath the town, so the plan is
compact and the geography is something the player can hold in their head: *the well in the
square is the top of the shaft she ends up at the bottom of.*

```
            N
   [Flume pier]                                        fallen span
        |                                                 ____
  W  [gate]==[MEETING]----- street -----[well-house]-----/ Z1  \=== culvert (start)   E
              HOUSE Z3        Z2          (exit, Z7)      gully
                 |                            ^
               hatch                          | lift
                 v                            |
              cloakroom, fan, stair Z4        |
                 |___ the Gallery Z5 (runs east, under the street) ___ great door __ the Sink Z6
```

### Z1 — The Culvert and the Flume Road (exterior)

- **Purpose.** First image, goal on the horizon, controls, the gun, the pursuit.
- **Size.** Gully 110 x 35 m, falling 8 m toward the town. Walls 6–10 m, so nothing is
  drawn beyond them but sky, fog cards and the Flume.
- **Landmark.** The Flume: celadon piers 55 m tall with big-radius shoulders, most of the
  deck gone, marching west into peach haze. One fallen span makes the black culvert the
  player starts inside. On the horizon, the Wick.
- **Light.** Palette B, "Long Light". The first twenty seconds are over-exposed toward
  palette A as the eye adjusts out of the culvert. Sun ahead and to the left, so every pier
  and post throws a long mauve shadow toward the player.
- **What the player does.** Walks out of the dark into the valley (the framed doorway
  shot). Reads a road sign: `TARRY — WATER — 40 CHAINS`, the 40 painted out, 55 painted out,
  80. Finds cold camp 1: a dead fire, a survey stake, a violet ribbon that streams west no
  matter where the wind is. Reaches the Stockgate: a heavy bar across the gully held by
  seven ceramic strike pegs with ochre rings. Each peg rings a note when shot. Seven pegs,
  six rounds: the gate teaches fire and reload without a prompt. A note is nailed to the post.
- **What the player learns.** Someone is ahead and is not hurrying. Distances here get
  longer. White ceramic with an ochre ring answers a bullet with a tone. She counts.

### Z2 — Tarry (exterior settlement)

- **Purpose.** The place the player will remember. First enemy, first puzzle, first fight.
- **Size.** One street, 90 x 55 m including the square. Nine frontier buildings, over-tall
  and leaning 2–4 degrees; only porches are enterable except the meeting house.
- **Landmarks.** The wind-pump frame in the square with three ceramic insulators hung as
  bells. The well-house: a 4 m celadon Pellam riser drum with a plank shed built round it,
  shut. The trough, full to the lip. The meeting house closing the west end of the street,
  with a Flume pier rising behind it and that pier's shadow lying the length of the street.
- **Light.** Palette B at full strength. Three-quarter back-light, warm rims, the street
  striped with porch-post shadows. No flame anywhere: every chimney is cold, every lamp
  unlit. (Flame means people. There is none until the last minute of the stage.)
- **What is wrong here.** Every porch is swept and no tracks come down from any of them.
  A broom leans by every door. The trough overflows a little, steadily, and the spill
  runs west.
- **What the player does.** Drinks (health). Finds the chalk drawing on the well-house
  planks: a child's picture of four circles in a row, big, small, middle, small, and stick
  figures walking toward a tall house. Sees the Sweeper on the meeting-house porch. Deals
  with the Sweeper. Shoots the meeting call on the bells. Survives the town coming to meeting.
- **What the player learns.** The enemies are the townsfolk. The plate is the thing to
  shoot. A plate shot and a body shot do not end the same way, and the street remembers
  which one was used.

### Z3 — The Meeting House (interior)

- **Purpose.** The quiet heart of the stage. The found account. The first toll.
- **Size.** One hall, 16 x 10 m, ridge at 7 m. Frontier plank and adobe on three sides.
  The fourth (west) wall is the bare ceramic face of the Pellam headworks, which the town
  built its hall around. The lectern is a Pellam console. The "altar step" is a 3 m ceramic
  hatch.
- **Landmark.** The north wall: a vote scratched in tally strokes at child height and
  adult height. Thirty-one on the left. Four on the right.
- **Light.** Palette B interior. Dark, warm brown-black. Three tall south windows are
  shuttered; hairlines of gold leak round them. One hairline of violet stands over the
  hatch. Exposure opens a stop on entry, so the door behind is a white abstract.
- **What the player does.** Puzzle 2 (Daylight). Reads the ledger (optional, six short
  entries). Finds cold camp 2: a chair drawn up to the console, a cup, a lamp burned dry.
  Shoots the knot with lead and hears it ring flat. Chambers a toll. Fires it.
- **What the player learns.** They voted. He only told them what it cost. The children were
  sent east before the vote. The Kept came up to sweep in the evenings, at first. A toll
  rings plumb, and there are now two.

### Z4 — Attendant Change (underground threshold)

- **Purpose.** The descent. The grade change the player feels in their stomach. The
  single scare.
- **Size.** Cloakroom 12 x 8 x 4.2 m. Fan housing 6 m across. Stairwell 5 x 5 m, 18 m
  deep, wrapped round the riser pipe. Pellam risers are 0.6 m, not built for people; the
  town has laid its own plank steps over them (collision is a ramp).
- **Landmark.** Thirty-five pegs in one row. Thirty-one have a coat, a hat, and boots set
  square beneath. Four are empty. The coats stir in the draught from the fan. One does not.
- **Light.** Palette B underground: cold, regular, aqua strips. The fan turns once in
  eight seconds and its shadow sweeps the coats (a rotating mask multiplied over the bake).
- **What the player does.** Hears the station speak for the first time (tones and
  subtitles). Solves Puzzle 3 (the Breath). Descends. Halfway down, the strip lights go out
  one by one from below, and when they return a Kept is standing in a wall niche at arm's
  length, plate lit aqua, off shift. It does not attack. Its plate turns to follow her.
- **What the player learns.** They hung up their coats like people going to work. The
  machine is not angry; it has a roll and a schedule. The Kept are not always hostile.

### Z5 — The Gallery (underground hall)

- **Purpose.** Scale. Two fights and the showpiece gun puzzle. The second toll.
- **Size.** About 72 x 22 m, 11 m high, running east back under the street. A 6 m canal
  down the middle. Near bank 7 m wide with a riser pillar every 12 m; far bank 9 m with a
  raised catwalk. The east third widens into the pump floor (24 x 22 m arena, two loops,
  six full-height pump casings as cover). The stair lets in through a corridor 1.8 m wide
  and 2.4 m high: compression, then release.
- **Landmark.** At the east end, the great door: a ceramic ring 7 m across, far too big
  for people, with a hairline of violet all the way round it. A single instanced row of
  lamps recedes to it. One lamp in eight flickers.
- **Light.** Near-black blue fog taking on aqua with distance. Satin floor, a smear of
  light under every lamp. The water is black with aqua streaks and it runs **west**,
  uphill, away from the well.
- **What the player does.** Watches the Ox across the water fit a ceramic panel into a gap
  in the wall and seat it with a slam, three Tenders holding work lamps for it. Finds the
  operator's stand and plays Six Caps (Puzzle 4). Fights the duel across the water. Crosses.
  Finds cold camp 3. Fights the Ox. Meets the picket at the great door and rings Toll 2.
- **What the player learns.** The station is running at full output and almost none of it
  goes up the well. How the Ox moves and where its back is, before it is a threat. That a
  toll can spare a crowd at once, and that there is now one.

### Z6 — The Sink (boss chamber)

- **Purpose.** The parley, the boss, the last toll.
- **Size.** Round, 30 m across, 14 m high. In the centre the Sink itself: an 8 m shaft of
  black water with the Governor's spindle rising out of it. A 10 m ring of floor. Six riser
  pillars at 9 m radius (cover, and each carries a strike plate). Four floor hatches. Two
  ammunition niches. The lift cage waits behind a shut gate marked `EGRESS`.
- **Landmark.** The Governor.
- **Light.** The only room where violet is allowed to fill the frame, always with a
  near-white core. Aqua work-lamps are dimmed to embers. When the last toll rings, violet
  dies everywhere at once and the room is plain aqua and very large.
- **What the player learns.** `OUTPUT: FULL. DELIVERY: WEST MAIN. TOWN SERVICE: ONE TROUGH.`
  The town was paid one trough for thirty-one people. The rest of the water is going west.

### Z7 — And Water After (exterior, blue hour; Z2 reused)

- **Purpose.** The ending image and the hook.
- **Size.** The same street, entered from the well-house in the square, plus 40 m of west
  road beyond the gate.
- **Light.** Palette C, "Blue Hour". No sun. An ember band on the horizon. The same
  geometry, relit; the Wick brighter than before and leaning a little further.
- **What the player does.** Rides the lift up. Steps out of the black well-house door: the
  opening shot's composition, later light. Counts windows. Fills her canteen while there is
  something to fill it from. Walks to the west gate. Reads the last note. Looks at the plain.

## 6. The puzzles

Shared rules (from `docs/research/game-feel.md` section 5): one-room rule; state always
legible by lamp and by sound; no combat inside a puzzle; a self-refilling cartridge box
within 10 m; hint tiers on 60 / 120 / 210 s of no progress, paused in combat, reset by any
correct step. Every puzzle is solvable with sound off and without colour vision.

### Puzzle 1 — The Meeting Call (Z2; observation, then shoot)

- **Premise.** The meeting-house doors are barred from inside. The town called itself to
  meeting with a tune on three bells hung on the wind-pump. The Kept still answer it.
- **Mechanics.** Three ceramic insulator bells, visibly big, middle and small, each with
  an ochre ring. A bullet rings a bell (low, middle, high) and swings it. On the meeting-house
  lintel are four dark lamps. A correct note lights the next lamp. A wrong note puts them
  all out with a soft descending clunk. Notes must be within 6 s of each other.
- **Solution.** Big, small, middle, small. Four rounds.
- **How it is taught.** The Stockgate already taught that ringed ceramic sings when shot.
  The chalk drawing on the well-house shows the four circles in order with people walking
  to the tall house. The bells chime at random in the wind, so the player has heard all
  three pitches before reading the drawing. The faint wellsong from the well-house repeats
  the same four notes every 20 s (captioned `[the well: low, high, middle, high]`).
- **Result.** The bar is lifted from inside. The doors open inward on darkness. Five
  Tenders walk out in a line, unhurried, as if to meeting, plates lit calm aqua. At 14 m the
  hairlines on the plates go violet. *Solve, then ambush*, with three full seconds in which
  the enemy is on show before it is a threat.
- **Hints.** T0: the drawing is on the planks beside the trough the player drinks from;
  lamps on the lintel face the square. T1 (60 s): the next correct bell catches the sun and
  swings on its own. T2 (120 s): *"The door wanted a tune. Some child had drawn it."*
  T3 (210 s): *"Big bell first."* and the bells outline-pulse in order. Fail-safe: after
  five wrong sequences, lit lamps no longer reset.

### Puzzle 2 — Daylight (Z3; environmental logic, three steps)

- **Premise.** Pellam headworks wake to daylight. The town shuttered the south windows the
  year the lectern first spoke, and hung a tin shrine door over the pale disc on the wall
  that the light used to reach. The console is asleep and the hatch has no power.
- **Mechanics.** Three shuttered windows, each held by one iron latch 4 m up. Shoot a
  latch and the shutter drops open: a blade of gold light slams across the hall and lands
  on the north wall. The low sun makes each landing spot specific.
  - Shutter 1 lights the tally wall: thirty-one strokes, four strokes.
  - Shutter 2 lights the Assessor's chair, his cup, and his note.
  - Shutter 3 is aimed at the day-cell, but the town's roll banner (family brands painted
    on cloth) hangs from a rafter in the way, and the day-cell is behind the shrine door.
- **Solution.** Shoot latch 3. Shoot the banner's cord. Shoot the shrine door's hasp.
  The shaft reaches the disc; the console wakes with a rising chime; aqua strips run along
  the headworks to the hatch rim. Three shots minimum, five if the player opens everything,
  which they should, because the first two shutters are the story.
- **How it is taught.** Hairlines of light already leak round each shutter and point at
  their landing spots. The latches are the only bright metal in the room. A Pellam plate
  beside the disc carries a pictogram: sun, arrow, disc, open door. "Wrong" shutters are
  never wrong: they do something beautiful and informative.
- **Then the knot.** With power on, the hatch should open. It does not. A surveyor's tripod
  stands over it with a plumb bob on violet ribbon, hanging a hand's width off the
  vertical, leaning west. Lead passes through the ribbon with a flat, dead ring and a
  violet spark. The toll prompt appears (see section 8).
- **Hints.** T1: the unbroken latch glints; dust in the existing hairline thickens toward
  the day-cell. T2: *"The lectern wanted daylight. The town had boarded it out."*
  T3: *"The west shutter. Then whatever stood in its way."* plus outline pulse on the next target.

### Puzzle 3 — The Breath (Z4; timing) — designated scope valve

- **Premise.** The way from the cloakroom to the stair is through the housing of the
  station's intake fan, which the town called the Breath. Three slow blades, one turn in
  eight seconds. It will not hurt her; it is simply in the way.
- **Mechanics.** Two brake strike plates sit on the far wall behind the fan, one left,
  one right, each visible only through a passing gap between blades (about 1.6 s open,
  1.0 s shut). Both must be struck within one turn of the fan. A shot that meets a blade
  sparks and clangs, and the fan shudders.
- **Solution.** Two shots, timed through two gaps. The fan winds down with a long sigh.
  The coats stop moving. All of them.
- **How it is taught.** `STRIKE BOTH TO BRAKE` pictogram on the housing. The sweeping
  shadow in the cloakroom has already made the player watch the rhythm for a minute.
- **Hints.** T1: each plate glints as its gap arrives. T2: *"It could be stopped from here,
  if she was patient."* T3: *"Both plates, inside one turn."* Fail-safe: after three blade
  hits the fan slows by 40 percent.

### Puzzle 4 — Six Caps (Z5; the cylinder as the constraint)

- **Premise.** The slab bridge over the canal is retracted. It runs on line pressure, held
  by six header caps on the far wall. Open all six in one breath and the pressure goes to
  the bridge ram. The caps reseat themselves.
- **Mechanics.** Six strike plates on the far wall at 14 to 34 m and three heights. Each
  hit lights a lamp above the cap and sounds the next note of a rising six-note scale. From
  the first hit the player has 8 s before the caps reseat (ratchet ticking, lamps dying one
  by one, a visible and audible reset). Pillars hide at least two caps from everywhere
  except one spot.
- **Solution.** Stand on the operator's stand and empty the cylinder: six notes, six
  lamps, the scale resolves into a chord and the bridge grinds out across the water. At
  0.48 s cadence six shots take 2.4 s, and a single-round reload takes under a second, so
  the window forgives one miss comfortably and two with a steady hand.
- **How it is taught.** The stand is a Pellam floor decal (two footprints) half under
  silt, with a bright wear patch, and the Assessor has driven a ribbon stake beside it: he
  stood here to pull the bridge in. Thick pipes run visibly from each cap to the bridge
  ram. The Stockgate and the bells have already taught that plates are notes.
- **Result.** The bridge. And, as it locks, three tall figures step onto the far catwalk
  and lower their staffs to level.
- **Hints.** T1: the stand's footprints light aqua. T2: *"Six caps. She carried six. She
  would need to see all of them at once."* T3: *"From the footprints. All six, without
  stopping."* Fail-safes: after three failed tries the window becomes 12 s (*"The caps were
  old. They seated slower each time."*); after five, struck caps stay open.

**Boss phase 2** reuses this language (strike the lit brake plate to force an opening), so
the boss examines only what the puzzles taught.

### The mercy rule (applies to both Kept types)

- The **plate** is the head hit volume (inflated 1.4x; 1.6x on Easy). One plate hit at any
  time: the plate shatters with a clear ceramic ring, the collar goes dark, and the person
  **sits down where they stand** and stays there, breathing, for the rest of the stage.
  Kept within 4 m flinch for 0.5 s at the sound, so mercy is also crowd control.
- A **body** hit does revolver damage as normal. Killed Kept are thrown back and lie.
- Seated people have no hit volume. The game does not offer that shot.
- Feedback differs by shape and sound as well as result: a plate break uses the weak-point
  marker and a bell-like "tink"; a kill uses the kill marker and the low thud.
- After a fight the arena is a record: some sitting, some lying. The narrator does not
  comment on the ratio. The end card does the counting.

### Tender (pressure)

- **Who.** A townsperson in work clothes: apron, long coat or skirt-and-shawl (three
  vertex-colour variants on one mesh). Ceramic collar, strike plate for a face.
- **Silhouette.** Low and hunched, about 1.4 m in posture, head forward, a short billhook
  or pipe wrench carried low. Dark shape, one pale disc.
- **Behaviour.** Walks until 14 m, then runs at 5.8 m/s. Lunge at 1.8 m, 18 damage.
  Silent except for breath and the plate's two-note chirp.
- **Telegraph.** 0.5 s crouch; the plate's hairline flares violet; rising chirp. 0.6 s
  recovery after a missed lunge.
- **Killed well.** One round anywhere ends it. Shot *well* means the plate: the crouch
  holds the head dead still, facing the player, for half a second at four metres. Waiting
  for it is the bravest and kindest shot in the game. The safer mercy is to side-step the
  lunge and take the plate from the side during recovery.

### Rodman (zoning)

- **Who.** A townsperson given a surveyor's levelling staff by the Assessor.
- **Silhouette.** Tall and thin, 1.9 m, with a 2.6 m banded staff held upright: a long
  vertical line. Underground the staff tip and the plate are the pale things.
- **Behaviour.** Holds 12–25 m. Moves between authored firing points at 3.5 m/s,
  relocating after two shots. Backs off inside 8 m. Side-steps once if stared at for 0.6 s
  beyond 10 m.
- **Attack and telegraph.** Swings the staff from vertical down to level over 0.9 s (the
  whole silhouette changes), tip brightening, tone rising. Then one violet bolt at 18 m/s,
  22 damage, bright and dodgeable. The head is still for the last 0.4 s of the aim.
- **Killed well.** Two body shots kill. Any hit during the aim breaks the aim: the
  revolver is a parry. One plate shot during the last 0.4 s of the aim frees them and
  cancels the bolt. It is the duellist's answer: let them level, then be faster.

### The Ox (anchor) — Pellam service jack

- **What.** A machine, never a person. A squat walking jack built to lift wall panels.
  Enamel white gone tea-stained, an aqua band at 1.2 m, a yoke bar across the shoulders.
- **Silhouette.** Wide T, 2.4 m tall, 1.6 m wide.
- **Behaviour.** Walks at 2.5 m/s toward the player. Slam (3.5 m radius, 38 damage) after
  a 1.0 s wind-up in which the yoke rises and a floor ring lights. Straight-line charge at
  9 m/s (35 damage) after a 0.8 s paw-and-tone; if it hits a pump casing it is stunned 2.0 s.
- **Voice.** Status lines as tones and subtitles: `OBSTRUCTION. CLEARING.`
  `PRESSURE LOW. CONTINUING.`
- **Killed well.** Panels are armour (grey spark, clank, quarter damage). The pressure
  accumulator on its back is a strike plate: three hits there kill it. It is exposed for
  1.5 s after every slam and for the whole stun after a charge into cover. Bait the charge
  past a casing, walk round, three rounds, done, with three left for the Tenders.
- **Why it is here.** It is the one thing in Tarry that was never anyone. The stage gives
  the player a fight with no arithmetic attached, at exactly the point they need one.

### Boss — the Governor of Lift Station Eight

- **What.** The station's regulating mind, and literally a governor: a 9 m spindle rising
  from the Sink with three arms, each carrying a ceramic weight the size of a cart. The
  faster it turns, the higher the arms fly and the tighter its sleeves close. When it slows
  it "hunts": the arms sag, the sleeves slide open, and the collar at each arm's root is
  exposed. Each collar is a strike plate. It is rigid machinery: no skinning.
- **Role.** The examiner. The fight is the cylinder made visible: a 5 s pattern (dodge and
  reload), then a 3.5 s hunt (six rounds into a collar).
- **The parley (30 s, player free to move, skipped on retry).**
  `LIFT STATION EIGHT. IN SERVICE.` / `ATTENDANT ROLL: THIRTY-ONE. ENROLLED BY CONSENT.` /
  `OUTPUT: FULL. DELIVERY: WEST MAIN. TOWN SERVICE: ONE TROUGH.` /
  `A NOTE IS HELD FOR THE VISITOR.` then, in the Assessor's hand: *"The water is sweet and
  they chose it. You will stop it anyway. It is what you are for."* /
  `VISITOR. YOU CARRY A STRIKING IMPLEMENT. STATE YOUR TRADE.`
  The fight starts on the player's first shot. Narration: *"She stated it."*
- **Attacks and telegraphs.**
  1. **Wash** (signature, 1.2 s). A 45-degree wedge of floor rims violet, tone climbs,
     a weight drops and drags through it. 38 damage. Leave the wedge.
  2. **Cast** (quick, 0.8 s). Three violet beads thrown 0.4 s apart at 16 m/s, 20 each.
     Side-step or put a pillar between.
  3. **Call** (phase 2 on, 1.5 s). Hatch lamps blink; two or three Kept climb out, shootable
     as they climb. They share one attack token while the Governor is mid-pattern.
  4. **Runaway** (phase 3, 1.0 s each). Two opposite wedges in sequence.
- **Phases.** One collar each, 8 plate hits per collar (24 in all). A phase checkpoint and
  a 3 s spectacle at each break: the freed weight tears loose and falls into the Sink.
  - *Phase 1, In Service.* Wash, Cast, hunt. It hunts by itself after each pattern.
  - *Phase 2, Out of Balance.* `HUNTING SUPPRESSED.` It no longer slows by itself. One of
    the six pillar brake plates lights aqua; strike it to force a 4 s hunt. Call begins.
  - *Phase 3, Runaway.* One weight left, the spindle whipping. The last collar is always
    open but orbiting; it slows in each forced hunt. Runaway wedges. The last of the roll climb out.
- **Fairness.** Health bar with three pips. No hit over 40. Every off-screen attack has a
  directional tone. After two deaths in a phase: damage x0.85 and an extra cartridge box,
  silently. Retry at the phase in under 3 s.
- **Killed well.** Count. Reload during the pattern, not during the hunt. Six into the
  collar, every hunt. And if a Tender comes up beside you in phase 3, break the plate, not
  the person: there is one more round after this one and the end card is still counting.
- **The kill is not a kill.** With the third collar gone the spindle grinds on, bare.
  `REGULATOR EXPOSED FOR SERVICE. EGRESS HELD WHILE STATION IN SERVICE.` The regulator
  rises out of the Sink wound in violet ribbon: the third knot. Any Kept still standing form
  a ring and wait. See section 10.

## 8. The special round: the toll

The stage's one special. No secondary weapon. (Fan-the-hammer is not part of this pitch;
if the GDD rules it a firing mode of the revolver rather than an ability, it drops in
unchanged and is thematically apt, since a fanned shot cannot break a plate.)

- **What it is.** A pale bell-metal cartridge. Three sit in loops on the HUD beside the
  cylinder, visible from the first second, unexplained until the meeting house.
- **Exact mechanics.** A toll can be chambered only when a knot is within 25 m and in line
  of sight. The loop on the HUD lifts; `Q` swaps one round for the toll (1.2 s, a distinct
  animation: the one pale cartridge among five dark). Anywhere else the key gives a quiet
  line, once: *"Not here. They were promised."* The next trigger pull fires it.
- **What it does.** It rings. A struck bell with a six-second tail, the only perfectly
  in-tune sound in a stage whose machines all sing slightly flat; everything else ducks
  under it. Time dips to 0.3x for 0.4 s. A pale ring races outward across every surface
  (one radial term in the world shader, like the muzzle pulse). Within 25 m every off-plumb
  thing breaks: the knot unties and its bob swings down to hang truly vertical; every
  violet hairline dies; **every Kept plate in range shatters at once and its wearer sits down.**
- **It cannot miss.** *"It did not need to hit. It needed to be heard."* The effect is the
  sound, centred on the muzzle. Three knots, three tolls, no free fire: the stage cannot be
  soft-locked.
- **The three uses teach by threes.** Toll 1 opens a door, alone, in a quiet room: this is
  what it is. Toll 2 spares six people at the great door: this is what it is *for*. Toll 3
  stops the Governor: this is what it costs.
- **Why it earns its place.** It is not there to add a combat verb; the revolver has
  enough. It is the stage's emotional through-line made into an object the player loads
  with their own hand: a visible, countable, irreplaceable thing that the player watches
  go from three to none while understanding, a little later than the Assessor did, why.
  It is also the cheapest spectacle in the build: one synthesized bell, one shader ring.

## 9. Five moments a player would describe to a friend

1. **"You ring the town to meeting."** You shoot a child's tune on three bells, the barred
   doors open from the inside, and the townspeople file out into the long light in ceramic
   masks, calmly, as if it were Sunday. Then the masks change colour.
2. **"Headshots are mercy."** Break the plate and the person sits down and breathes. After
   every fight you can count who is sitting and who is lying, and at the end the windows
   light up for the ones who made it home.
3. **"Every shot in the church was a sunbeam."** The meeting house is black until you
   shoot the shutter latches, and each one throws a blade of gold across the room: onto
   the vote, onto his empty chair, onto the machine's sleeping face.
4. **"Thirty-one coats."** A changing room under the town with the whole population's
   coats hung on pegs and their boots set square, a slow fan's shadow sweeping across
   them, and one coat that is not moving.
5. **"The last bullet."** The boss is beaten and still running, the exit is held, and you
   have one bell round you have been saving the entire game for someone else. You load it.
   It rings. Every machine stops. And when you come up it is evening, and his campfire,
   which has been a day ahead for eleven years, is right there, and it is not moving.

Runner-up: the duel across the black water, where the only warm light in a hall the size
of a cathedral is your own muzzle flash, and each shot is a photograph of three tall
figures lowering their staffs.

## 10. The ending beat and the hook

**The last toll.** Third collar gone. The Governor grinds on. The regulator stands out of
the water wrapped in violet. The lift gate reads `EGRESS HELD WHILE STATION IN SERVICE`.
Lead rings flat. One loop on the HUD lifts. There is no other way out of the room and the
player knows what the round was for.

*"One was for him. It had always been for him."*

She fires. The bell. Violet dies in every corner at once. The spindle coasts down over
eight seconds, its tone sliding to nothing, and the wellsong that has been under every
minute of the stage since the street **stops**. Four seconds of true silence. Any Kept
still standing sit down. `STATION EIGHT. OUT OF SERVICE. ATTENDANT ROLL: RELEASED.
THANK YOU FOR YOUR HOURS.` The water in the Sink goes still, then begins, visibly, to drop.

**The lift.** *"The lift took longer going up than the stair had taken going down. She
counted the lamps. There were forty more."*

**Blue hour.** She steps out of the black well-house door into the street: the opening
shot's framing, looking west. It was late afternoon when she went down. It is evening now,
and not the same evening. Windows are lit along the street, flame-coloured, the first
flame in the whole stage, one for every share of the roll that came home. A figure sits on
a porch with its back to her and its hat low, a broken plate laid neatly on the step
beside the broom. Every ribbon in town has gone grey. The trough is half down.

*"She filled her canteen while there was something to fill it from."*

**The gate.** The west gate stands open, bar on the ground. Pinned to the post, in his
hand: *"Three, for a town that never asked you. I would have thought less of you otherwise.
Now we can talk. Shade to you, Rider."*

**The hook.** Past the gate the plain opens. The Wick is brighter, and leaning further than
it did this afternoon. And out on the flat, close, is one small orange fire with something
pale and upright beside it that might be a man or might be a stake.

*"For eleven years his fire had been a day ahead. Tonight it was forty chains off, and it
was not moving."*

Hold four seconds. Card: **AND WATER AFTER.** Then the stats:
*On the roll: 31. Came home: n. Tolls: none. Rounds fired. Plates broken. Time.*

Why it earns the next stage: the pursuit has inverted. She spent the only thing that could
end him, he arranged it, and now, for the first time, he is waiting. The player leaves
with three questions and no weapon for any of them: what does he want to say, where is the
water going, and why is the Wick leaning.

## 13. Risks to build (honest list)

1. **The mercy rule is the pitch, and it lives or dies on a head hitbox.** A hunched
   target running at 5.8 m/s with a 0.17 m plate is a hard shot. If plate shots are not
   reliably makeable in the crouch window and the lunge recovery, the end card becomes a
   scolding and the stage's best idea becomes its worst. Needs early tuning through the
   step hook (hitbox scale, crouch hold time) and an Easy-mode inflation. Fallback: the
   crouch hold lengthens to 0.65 s.
2. **Tone: the player shoots townspeople.** This is deliberate and it is the inverse of
   the first book's massacre-in-a-town beat, not a restaging: there is no preacher, the
   town consented by vote, and the design's whole pressure is toward *not* killing them.
   But it sits near a signature set-piece listed in `art-tone.md` 1.6, and it can tip into
   grimness if the narration moralises or the bodies are lurid. Guards: no gore, the
   narrator never comments on the count, seated survivors cannot be shot, children are
   explicitly sent away in the ledger. Someone should review this against the
   homage-not-copy rule before the GDD locks.
3. **Scripted humanoids in clothing.** Coats, aprons and skirts authored in Blender Python
   are harder to make look good than machines. The plate removes faces; one rig serves
   both types; long garments hide legs and forgive a simple walk. Still the largest art risk.
4. **Dynamic sun shafts over a baked room (Puzzle 2).** Shutters that open at runtime need
   shaft cards plus additive hot-patch decals faking the landed light, since the lightmap
   cannot change. If it looks pasted on, the meeting house loses its moment. Fallback: the
   room's bake includes all three shafts and the shutters are simply occluders drawn over
   the windows with the shaft cards hidden, which is cheaper but less dramatic.
5. **Blue-hour reuse of the street.** Needs a second lighting state. Proposal: lightmap
   for the day bake, a second vertex-colour set for dusk (dusk has no sun, so low-frequency
   vertex light should hold). Lit windows are unlit quads with glow sprites, so there is no
   baked spill from them. Fallback: sky, fog and grade swap over the day bake, accepting
   wrong shadows.
6. **A lot rests on text and tones.** No voice-over means the station's "voice" is chimes
   plus subtitles and the Assessor is five notes. Too many cards during movement will be
   missed; too few and the vote and the water never land. The ledger is optional reading,
   so the critical facts (they voted; one trough; the water goes west) must also arrive by
   the tally wall and the parley. Needs a blind read-through by a critic.
7. **Puzzle 1 is audio-flavoured.** It must be fully solvable from the drawing and bell
   sizes alone, with captions. Puzzle 3 is thin and is the designated cut.
8. **The toll is a key that lives in the cylinder, not a combat ability.** Players who
   want a second verb will not get one. It cannot be free-fired, and the refusal line has
   to feel like character, not like a locked button. If judges want more systemic use, the
   plate-shatter flinch (4 m, 0.5 s) is the place to grow it, not the toll.
9. **The boss is the most complex script in the stage.** Three phases, orbiting hit
   targets in phase 3, floor-wedge telegraphs, adds from hatches, a brake-plate twist, and
   a scripted finale that must not be skippable by accident or stallable. Phase 2's twist
   is the first cut. Hitscan on a fast-orbiting collar needs a generous volume.
10. **The roll must stay at exactly 31.** No encounter may spawn extra Kept for pacing or
    ammunition, and every one must be reachable by the final toll or accounted for, or the
    end card lies. This constrains the AI and encounter owners and needs a test.
11. **Name and collision checks are shallow.** Coined terms were checked once against the
    series' official glossary through an automated summary, not read line by line, and
    "Pellam Deepworks" has had no trademark check (inherited from the art brief).
    "Assize" is real English and may need a line of in-world gloss.
12. **Persistent bodies cost draw calls.** Seated and lying Kept should freeze to static
    posed meshes once settled; up to eleven remain in the Sink. Within budget on paper,
    unmeasured.
13. **Length sits at the top of the range** (24 minutes median as written). The cut order
    in section 4 brings it to about 21.
